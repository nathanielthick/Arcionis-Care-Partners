Community Living Supports in Redding, California

Community Living Supports (CLS) is nonmedical, one-on-one help with daily routines and community activities, for people with developmental disabilities who want to take part in everyday life in the ways they choose.

This program is pending approval from Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC). Contact us to check its current status. We're happy to answer questions in the meantime.

Questions? Call (530) 781-6701 or send us a request

What are Community Living Supports?

Help with everyday life, at home and out in the community

We start by asking what matters to the person, what they already do, what gets in the way, and how they want help. A person might want to shop for a familiar meal, keep meeting a friend, or attend a community class.

Our staff member might prepare materials, help with communication, go along, give a prompt, or do the part of a task the person wants help with. The person sets the pace and does the parts they want and can do.

CLS is arranged through Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC) as personal assistance. It is separate from respite, supported living, day programs, independent living instruction, and clinical behavioral treatment.

Keeping up a routine that matters, with ongoing help, counts as success too.

Who it's for

  • People age 5 and older, with no upper age limit
  • Referred by their Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC) service coordinator
  • Whose IPP identifies a need for personal assistance
  • Whose support needs can be met safely with nonmedical help
Our approach

Taking part in community life, at a comfortable pace

We help people with developmental disabilities take part in their community, keep relationships that matter, and maintain the living arrangement they want. Support follows each person's interests, preferences, and goals.

Before outings, we check the plan together: the person's choice, how to ask for help or a break, the place, weather and smoke, traffic and crowds, restrooms, costs, and the way home. If an activity isn't safe that day, we adapt or postpone it with the person.

What we focus on

  • Community participationSupport with chosen activities, local outings, classes, and time with friends.
  • Everyday tasksHelp with meals, getting ready, and other daily tasks the plan authorizes.
  • Individual choiceThe person can change their mind, rest, or stop at any time.
  • Outing safetyA safety check before every outing, and a safe handoff at the end.
What's included

What we do, and our care limits

We review every person's needs before services start. If our service isn't the right fit, we'll tell you clearly and help you and your service coordinator look at other options.

What CLS can include

  • One-on-one help with daily tasks at home, like preparing a meal or getting ready
  • Going along to stores, libraries, parks, recreation sites, and community classes
  • Prompts, picture steps, showing how, or hands-on help with the parts of a task the person wants help with
  • Going along on a walk, on public transit, or on a family or other approved ride
  • Help handling money for an activity when specifically authorized, with receipts kept and change returned
  • Medication reminders, and opening packaging a caregiver has already prepared, with written authorization
  • Toileting or hygiene help when two trained ACP staff are on site and FNRC has given written authorization for the task and staffing

Care limits: what we can't do

  • Driving participants. ACP staff don't drive people to activities.
  • Staff-assisted wheelchair mobility, lifts, transfers that need lifting, or specialized transport
  • Choosing, measuring, or giving medication, or any nursing or medical care
  • Dementia-specific care, including care for Alzheimer's disease
  • Physical or mechanical restraint, seclusion, or punishment
  • Clinical behavioral treatment or crisis support. If someone is in danger or needs urgent medical help, call 911.
  • Overnight care. Visits end by 9:00 p.m.
Where and when

Service area and hours

Redding, CA, and eligible locations in Shasta and Tehama counties within about a 30-minute drive of Redding. Coverage depends on the exact location and travel time. Send us your address or general area and we will confirm.

Visits are available Monday through Sunday, 8:30 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., at home and in ordinary community places like stores, libraries, parks, and recreation sites. We take intake and scheduling calls daily from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., and a supervisor is reachable during every visit. We explain any activity costs ahead of time.

Whether we can start depends on your location, your loved one's needs, written authorization, and which trained staff are available.

How referrals work

  1. Ask us anything. Call (530) 781-6701 or email acp.info@arcioniscarepartners.com. We'll explain the program and its current status, and help you connect with your FNRC service coordinator.
  2. Get a referral. Formal referrals come through your FNRC service coordinator. We aim to meet within two to three business days of receiving enough information.
  3. Plan together. We complete a support assessment and plan with the person: the activities and tasks they choose, how they want help, and safety and travel arrangements.
  4. Authorization and staff match. We confirm FNRC's written authorization, then prepare a trained staff member for the person's plan.
  5. Start and review. We check how the first two visits went and review progress with the person at least every three months.
Questions

Common questions

More answers about referrals, the Self-Determination Program, and getting started are in our full FAQ.

How do I get Community Living Supports?

Through a referral from your FNRC service coordinator. Call us first with questions if you like, and we'll help you connect with your coordinator. If you use the Self-Determination Program (SDP), ask your coordinator or Independent Facilitator (IF) about your options.

Is it available now?

This program is pending approval from FNRC. Call us to check its current status. We can still answer questions and help you talk with your service coordinator.

How is CLS different from respite?

Respite is mainly a break for the caregiver. CLS is help for the person to do the daily routines and community activities they choose. They are separate programs.

Who pays for activity costs?

We explain any costs, like admission or a meal, before the activity and confirm how the person chooses to pay.

Can activities change over time?

Yes. Tell us when interests or goals change, and we'll review the plan with the person and their planning team before adding something new.

How do I raise a concern?

Tell any staff member, call (530) 781-6701, or email us. We acknowledge concerns within two business days and aim to respond in writing within five business days. You can also contact your FNRC service coordinator or the Clients' Rights Advocate at (530) 345-4113 at any time.
