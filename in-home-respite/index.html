In-Home Respite in Redding, California

Respite means a short break for the family. A trained Arcionis Care Partners (ACP) staff member comes to your home and cares for your loved one, so you can rest, run errands, or go to appointments.

Questions? Call (530) 781-6701 or send us a request

What is in-home respite?

Planned breaks for caregivers, at home

In-home respite gives the people who usually provide care, such as parents or family members, regular time off. While you're away, our staff member supervises your loved one and follows their normal routines and activities.

You don't have to be in crisis to ask for respite. It can be scheduled regularly so caregiver stress doesn't build up.

Our respite is temporary, nonmedical care. That means we provide supervision, company, and help with daily routines, but not nursing or medical care. Staff follow your loved one's routines, communication preferences, and any authorized behavioral supports.

Before scheduling, we explain our medication, mobility, and personal-care limits so you know exactly what a visit can include.

Our approach

Rest for caregivers, comfort for your loved one

Family caregivers need dependable time to rest, recharge, and handle everyday responsibilities. Our planned respite visits keep your loved one's familiar routines, preferences, and activities going while you're away.

Our trauma-informed approach puts dignity, comfort, and positive relationships first. Trauma-informed means we keep in mind that hard past experiences can shape how a person reacts, so we focus on safety, trust, and choice. Before visits begin, we learn how your loved one communicates, what support they need, and what their day usually looks like.

What we focus on

  • Caregiver reliefScheduled support that gives family caregivers time for themselves.
  • Familiar routinesContinuity with the activities and support strategies your loved one knows.
  • Meaningful engagementActivities tailored to individual interests and preferences.
  • Family collaborationClear communication about expectations, needs, and ongoing support.
What's included

What we do, and our care limits

We review every person's needs before services start. If our service isn't the right fit, we'll tell you clearly and point you toward other options when we can.

What our staff can do

  • Supervise and keep your loved one company
  • Follow familiar routines, like meals, play, and quiet time
  • Do activities your loved one enjoys
  • Remind your loved one to take medicine they take on their own
  • With your written permission ahead of time, help open medicine packaging you prepared
  • Toileting prompts or help, and personal hygiene care, when two trained ACP staff are on site and you've given written authorization (see limits)

Care limits: what we can't do

  • Overnight care
  • Care for someone who uses a wheelchair or needs a special medical device to get around. The person must be able to walk (be ambulatory).
  • Lifting, or moving someone by hand or with a lift machine
  • Toileting or intimate hygiene care with only one staff member, or without written authorization. This care always needs two trained ACP staff on site.
  • Choosing, measuring, or giving medicine
  • Nursing or medical procedures, such as tube feeding, injections, or wound care
  • Emergency response. If someone is in danger or needs medical help, call 911.
Where we serve

Service area

Redding, CA, and eligible locations in Shasta and Tehama counties within about a 30-minute drive of Redding. Coverage depends on the exact location and travel time. Send us your address or general area and we will confirm.

We provide respite in the home where your loved one lives.

Whether we can start depends on your location, your loved one's needs, payment approval, and which trained staff are available.

How to get a referral

  1. Contact us. Call (530) 781-6701 or email acp.info@arcioniscarepartners.com. Share your name, how to reach you, your general area, and that you want in-home respite. Please don't send medical records in your first email.
  2. Talk about fit. We'll ask about mobility, personal care, communication, and medicine needs, and the days and times you want.
  3. Set up payment. If you're in SDP: ask your service coordinator or Independent Facilitator (IF) to add ACP respite to your SDP spending plan. We'll give your team the service details they need.
    If you use traditional regional center services: your service coordinator needs to make a formal referral and confirm authorization.
    Other arrangements: ask us about current options.
  4. Intake visit. We meet with you to learn your loved one's routines, likes, triggers, and care instructions, and go over the paperwork.
  5. Start respite. When authorization and a trained staff member are in place, we agree on a schedule and begin. You can ask us to review the plan any time needs change.
Questions

Common questions

More answers about funding, the Self-Determination Program, and getting started are in our full FAQ.

Can I use respite for a few hours a week?

Visits are scheduled within the hours your plan or authorization allows. Tell us what you're hoping for and we'll talk through what's possible.

Is respite the same as IHSS?

No. In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) is a county program that pays for certain care at home. Respite is a regional center or SDP service meant to give caregivers a break. Some families use both. Your team will check that services don't overlap.

What if my loved one has a hard day during a visit?

Our staff are trained in PCMA Everyday BehaviorTools and Ukeru, which focus on preventing and calming hard moments. We'll talk through what helps your loved one during intake, and staff follow any authorized behavior supports.
