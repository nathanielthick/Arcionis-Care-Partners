In-home respite with behavioral support

In-Home Respite in Redding, California

Respite is a planned break for the people who usually provide care. A trained Arcionis Care Partners (ACP) staff member comes to the home and supports your loved one, including the behavioral support their plan calls for, while you rest or take care of other things.

Questions? Call (530) 781-6701 or send us a request

What is in-home respite?

Planned breaks for caregivers, with behavioral support

In-home respite is temporary, nonmedical care and supervision in the family home or another approved home. It gives the usual caregiver time to rest, handle responsibilities, or take a planned break.

Our program is designed for people whose Individual Program Plan (IPP) calls for specialized behavioral support during respite. Staff follow your loved one's routines, communication preferences, and the behavioral supports in their plan.

We start by asking your loved one what they want from respite and what helps them feel safe. When the person wants to keep living at home and the family wants that too, helping make that possible is a central goal.

You don't have to be in crisis to ask for respite. It can be scheduled regularly so caregiver stress doesn't build up.

Who it's for

  • People age 5 and older, with no upper age limit, who live with family
  • Referred by their Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC) service coordinator
  • Whose IPP identifies a need for specialized behavioral support during respite
  • Whose needs can be met safely with nonmedical care and a qualified staff match
Our approach

Rest for caregivers, comfort for your loved one

Our trauma-informed approach puts dignity, comfort, and positive relationships first. Trauma-informed means we keep in mind that hard past experiences can shape how a person reacts, so we focus on safety, trust, and choice.

At intake, we ask your loved one what they'd like to get from respite, and we ask you, separately, what respite should make possible for you. We check in on both at least twice a year, and sooner when needs change.

What we focus on

  • Caregiver reliefDependable, scheduled time for family caregivers to rest and recharge.
  • Familiar routinesThe activities, routines, and support strategies your loved one already knows.
  • Choice and communicationStaff use your loved one's preferred way of communicating, offer choices, and respect a no.
  • Wellness in everyday routinesEnjoyable movement, rest, snacks, and drinks, within existing health and diet instructions.
What's included

What we do, and our care limits

We review every person's needs before services start. If our service isn't the right fit, we'll tell you clearly and help you and your service coordinator look at other options.

What our staff can do

  • Supervise and support your loved one one-on-one: one staff member for one person
  • Follow familiar routines, like meals, play, rest, and quiet time
  • Use the prevention and calming strategies in your loved one's behavior support plan
  • Remind your loved one to take medication they take on their own
  • With specific written authorization, open medication packaging you've already prepared
  • Toileting prompts or help, and intimate hygiene care, when two trained ACP staff are on site and FNRC has given written authorization for the task and the two-person staffing

Care limits: what we can't do

  • Overnight care. Visits end by 9:00 p.m.
  • Staff-assisted wheelchair mobility, lifting someone from a bed or other surface, transfers that need lifting, or using a mechanical lift
  • Driving your loved one. Staff don't transport people in staff or agency vehicles.
  • Toileting or intimate hygiene care with only one staff member, or without written FNRC authorization
  • Choosing, measuring, preparing, or giving medication
  • Skilled nursing, other medical care, or dementia-specific care, including care for Alzheimer's disease
  • Physical or mechanical restraint, seclusion, or using medication to control behavior
  • Clinical treatment, crisis services, or emergency response. If someone is in danger or needs urgent medical help, call 911.
Where and when

Service area and hours

Redding, CA, and eligible locations in Shasta and Tehama counties within about a 30-minute drive of Redding. Coverage depends on the exact location and travel time. Send us your address or general area and we will confirm.

Respite visits are available Monday through Sunday, 8:30 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., in the family home or another approved home. We take intake and scheduling calls daily from 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., and an on-duty contact is available during every visit.

Whether we can start depends on your location, your loved one's needs, written authorization, and which trained staff are available.

How referrals work

  1. Ask us anything. Call (530) 781-6701 or email acp.info@arcioniscarepartners.com. We'll explain the program and help you connect with your FNRC service coordinator. Please don't send medical records in your first message.
  2. Get a referral. Formal referrals come through your FNRC service coordinator. Ask them to refer your loved one to ACP for in-home respite. We aim to complete our first review within two to three business days of receiving the referral.
  3. Intake and safety planning. We meet with you and your loved one to learn their routines, communication, health and safety needs, and what helps on a hard day. We review the current IPP and behavior plan.
  4. Authorization and staff match. We confirm FNRC's written authorization, match a trained staff member to your loved one, and prepare a shift plan with you.
  5. Start respite. We agree on a schedule and begin. We review the plan with you at least twice a year, and sooner whenever needs change.
Questions

Common questions

More answers about referrals, the Self-Determination Program, and getting started are in our full FAQ.

Do we need a referral from the regional center?

Yes. Formal referrals for ACP respite come from your FNRC service coordinator. You're welcome to call us first with questions, and we'll help you connect with your coordinator.

Can respite cover two siblings at the same time?

Sometimes. We always assign one staff member to each person, never one staff member for two. Supporting siblings at the same visit needs written approval from FNRC and our Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA), plus a written safety plan.

What if my loved one has a hard day during a visit?

Staff use the prevention and calming strategies in your loved one's plan. Our staff train in PCMA Everyday BehaviorTools and Ukeru, and we don't use restraint or seclusion. If anyone is in danger, staff call 911 and then let you know.

Is respite the same as IHSS?

No. In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) is a county program that pays for certain care at home. Respite is a regional center service meant to give caregivers a break. Some families use both, and your team will check that services don't overlap.

How do I raise a concern?

Tell any staff member, call (530) 781-6701, or email us. We acknowledge concerns within two business days and aim to respond in writing within five business days. You can also contact your FNRC service coordinator or the Clients' Rights Advocate at (530) 345-4113 at any time.
