In-Home Behavior Intervention in Redding, California

Behavior intervention helps when certain behaviors make daily life hard or unsafe. We work in your home with your loved one and your family, with oversight from a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA).

Questions? Call (530) 781-6701 or send us a request

What is behavior intervention?

Understanding behavior, and building a plan that helps

All behavior is a way of communicating. Behavior intervention looks at why hard moments happen, such as pain, stress, noise, changes in routine, or trouble expressing needs, and then builds a plan to help prevent them and respond calmly.

This is scheduled assessment and treatment led by our Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA), a clinician certified in behavior analysis. The BCBA develops and reviews the plan, and qualified technicians carry out the assigned strategies. Our staff use PCMA Everyday BehaviorTools and Ukeru, two trauma-informed approaches that focus on prevention, communication, and the least restrictive response possible.

Goals may include communicating needs, reducing distress, and making daily routines safer.

We also coach parents and caregivers, so the same strategies can be used when we're not there.

Our approach

Scheduled behavioral support and stabilization, at home

We provide scheduled, trauma-informed behavioral services, including crisis prevention and stabilization support. Stabilization means helping things become calm and steady again after a hard period. Trauma-informed means we keep in mind that hard past experiences can shape how a person reacts, so we focus on safety, trust, and choice.

With oversight from our Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA), we work with individuals, families, and support teams to understand distress and build individual support strategies. Our approach uses PCMA Everyday BehaviorTools and Ukeru, with a focus on prevention, communication, de-escalation, and the least restrictive support that works for the person.

What we focus on

  • Prevention planningIdentifying triggers, stressors in the environment, and early signs of distress.
  • Individualized strategiesSupport guided by the person's needs and their written plan.
  • De-escalationResponses that put comfort, dignity, and safety first.
  • Caregiver trainingPractical tools so families and support teams respond the same way.
What's included

What we do, and our care limits

We review every person's needs before services start. If our service isn't the right fit, we'll tell you clearly and point you toward other options when we can.

What behavior intervention includes

  • Learning what triggers hard moments and the early warning signs
  • Changes to the home routine or environment that lower stress
  • A written, individual support plan reviewed by our BCBA
  • Calm, safe de-escalation (helping someone settle down) when things get hard
  • Hands-on coaching for parents, caregivers, and support teams
  • Regular progress check-ins

Care limits: what we can't do

  • Emergency response. We do not send staff in an emergency. If someone is in danger, call 911.
  • Medical or psychiatric treatment, or prescribing medicine
  • Choosing, measuring, or giving medicine
  • Lifting, or moving someone by hand or with a lift machine
  • Overnight care
Where we serve

Service area

Redding, CA, and eligible locations in Shasta and Tehama counties within about a 30-minute drive of Redding. Coverage depends on the exact location and travel time. Send us your address or general area and we will confirm.

Sessions take place where your loved one lives, so strategies fit real daily routines.

Whether we can start depends on your location, your loved one's needs, payment approval, and which trained staff are available.

How to get a referral

  1. Contact us. Call (530) 781-6701 or email acp.info@arcioniscarepartners.com. Tell us your name, how to reach you, your general area, and that you're asking about behavior intervention.
  2. Talk about what's happening. We'll ask, in general terms, what behaviors are a concern, how often they happen, and who else is involved, such as school or other providers.
  3. Get authorization. If you're in SDP: ask your service coordinator or Independent Facilitator (IF) to add the service to your spending plan. We'll give your team the service details they need.
    If you use traditional regional center services: your service coordinator needs to make a formal referral and confirm authorization.
  4. Assessment. Our BCBA meets with your family to observe and gather information. Please share any existing behavior plan or reports at this point.
  5. Plan and begin. Our BCBA develops an individual plan with you, qualified staff carry out the assigned strategies, and we help train the people who will use them. The plan is reviewed as things change.
Questions

Common questions

More answers about funding, the Self-Determination Program, and getting started are in our full FAQ.

Is this an emergency crisis service?

No. This is planned support, booked ahead of time, that aims to prevent crises. It does not include emergency response. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

Do you work with schools or other providers?

Yes. With your written permission, we can share strategies with teachers, other providers, and your Independent Facilitator (IF) so everyone responds the same way.
