Applied behavior analysis (ABA)

In-Home Behavior Intervention in Redding, California

Scheduled, in-home applied behavior analysis (ABA) led by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA), for people whose clinical needs call for more intensive behavioral support. We build useful skills and help the person stay safe in the living arrangement they choose.

This program is pending approval from Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC). Contact us to check its current status. We're happy to answer questions in the meantime.

Questions? Call (530) 781-6701 or send us a request

What is behavior intervention?

Understanding behavior, and building a plan that helps

All behavior is a way of communicating. Behavior intervention looks at why hard moments happen, such as pain, stress, noise, changes in routine, or trouble expressing needs. It then builds a plan to help prevent them and to teach skills that work better for the person.

Our approach is applied behavior analysis (ABA). Our BCBA assesses behavior, writes and reviews the plan, and supervises the work directly. Qualified behavior technicians carry out the assigned strategies during visits.

Goals are agreed with the person and their planning team. They may include communicating pain, asking for a break, joining a chosen routine with less distress, preventing injury, or helping supporters use a strategy reliably.

Visits are scheduled, including times when the person is calm, because that's often when new skills are easiest to learn.

Who it's for

  • People age 5 and older, with no upper age limit
  • Referred by their Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC) service coordinator
  • Whose IPP identifies a need for clinical behavioral services
  • Facing serious aggression, self-injury, leaving a safe area, or distress that gets in the way of safety, daily routines, or where they choose to live
Our approach

How treatment works

Treatment starts with a functional behavior assessment. Our BCBA reviews records, talks with the person and the people who support them, and observes, to understand what a behavior is doing for the person. The BCBA then writes an Individual Intervention Plan tied to the IPP and shares it with FNRC for team review.

We go at the person's pace, offer choices, and respect a no. We protect access to food, drink, rest, and communication throughout treatment, and the BCBA approves any change before staff use it.

What we focus on

  • AssessmentA functional behavior assessment to understand why hard moments happen.
  • Individual planGoals, teaching steps, and safety limits, written by the BCBA and tied to the IPP.
  • Supervised visitsTechnicians carry out the plan under the BCBA's direct supervision.
  • Caregiver coachingPractice with the strategies in your plan, in your language and on your schedule.
  • Progress reviewsA progress review for the planning team at least every six months.
What's included

What we do, and our care limits

We review every person's needs before services start. If our service isn't the right fit, we'll tell you clearly and help you and your service coordinator look at other options.

What behavior intervention includes

  • A functional behavior assessment by our BCBA
  • A written Individual Intervention Plan with goals, a starting point, and how progress is measured
  • Scheduled visits in the home, usually one staff member for one person
  • Teaching skills such as asking for a break or communicating pain
  • Coaching for parents and caregivers, tied to your loved one's actual plan
  • Progress reviews for the planning team at least every six months
  • Toileting or hygiene support only when the clinical plan includes it, with two trained ACP staff on site and written FNRC authorization for the task and staffing

Care limits: what we can't do

  • Emergency response or 24-hour crisis service. If someone is in immediate danger or needs urgent medical help, call 911.
  • Caregiver relief, respite, day care, school services, or routine community help. Those are separate services.
  • Medical or psychiatric treatment, or giving medication
  • Dementia-specific care, skilled nursing, or other care that needs licensed medical staff
  • Staff-assisted wheelchair mobility, lifts, transfers that need lifting, or transporting participants
  • Seclusion, mechanical or chemical restraint, face-down (prone) restraint, or punishment
Where and when

Service area and hours

Redding, CA, and eligible locations in Shasta and Tehama counties within about a 30-minute drive of Redding. Coverage depends on the exact location and travel time. Send us your address or general area and we will confirm.

Visits are scheduled Monday through Sunday by agreement, within the BCBA's availability and your authorized hours. They usually take place in the person's home; another setting needs a clinical reason and approval. This is not a 24-hour emergency service.

Whether we can start depends on your location, your loved one's needs, written authorization, and which trained staff are available.

How referrals work

  1. Ask us anything. Call (530) 781-6701 or email acp.info@arcioniscarepartners.com. We'll explain the program and its current status, and help you connect with your FNRC service coordinator.
  2. Get a referral. Formal referrals come through your FNRC service coordinator. Ask them how FNRC's Behavior Consultation Orientation applies to your family.
  3. Assessment. Our BCBA reviews records, meets with you and your loved one, and observes. Please share any existing behavior plan or reports.
  4. Plan and authorization. The BCBA writes an Individual Intervention Plan with you and sends it to FNRC for team review. We confirm consent and written authorization before treatment starts.
  5. Treatment and review. Qualified staff carry out the plan under the BCBA's supervision. We review progress with the planning team at least every six months.
Questions

Common questions

More answers about referrals, the Self-Determination Program, and getting started are in our full FAQ.

Is this ABA therapy?

Yes. In-home behavior intervention is applied behavior analysis (ABA), led by our Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) with supervised behavior technicians. It is arranged through Far Northern Regional Center (FNRC), so ask your service coordinator about a referral.

Is it available now?

This program is pending approval from FNRC. Call us to check its current status. We can still answer questions and help you talk with your service coordinator.

Is this an emergency crisis service?

No. Visits are planned ahead and aim to prevent crises. We don't provide emergency response. If someone is in immediate danger or needs urgent medical help, call 911.

Do parents have to take part?

Caregiver coaching helps strategies carry over, and we work around your schedule and access needs. But a child's access to behavior intervention can't be denied or delayed because a parent isn't able to take part.

Do you work with schools or other providers?

Yes. With your written permission, we can share strategies with teachers, other providers, and your planning team so everyone responds the same way.

How do I raise a concern?

Tell any staff member, call (530) 781-6701, or email us. We acknowledge concerns within two business days and aim to respond in writing within five business days. You can also contact your FNRC service coordinator or the Clients' Rights Advocate at (530) 345-4113 at any time.
